I've spent a lot of this year building a small research hub on this site. It
collects things I've written and published — my CV, a few project write-ups,
some research digests — and puts them in one place that I actually control,
instead of scattering them across wherever I happened to be working at the time.
That part is mostly plumbing: there's a content contract, a couple of satellite
repositories, and a build that stitches it all together. It works, and I'm happy
with it, but it isn't the reason I'm writing this.

The reason is that the hub only holds finished things. A CV, a paper, a project
page — by the time any of that is public it's been polished into something
formal, and the interesting part, the part where I was actually thinking, is
gone. This blog is the other half. It's where I put down what I'm reading, what
I'm building, and what I'm currently arguing with myself about, before any of it
is clean enough to file away somewhere.

Hence the name. "Rabbit hole" has a bad reputation — it sounds like a warning,
like time wasted — but going down one is most of how I learn anything. I read a
paper because it cited another paper, and an hour later I'm three levels down
reading about something only loosely related, and that detour turns out to be
the useful part. I want a place to leave a note when that happens, while it's
still fresh, rather than trying to reconstruct it later for a formal write-up.

So: the topics here will wander, on purpose. I'm not going to organize this into
categories, because I don't actually think in categories. Some weeks it's
software engineering, some weeks it's a paper on how language models reason, some
weeks it's whatever I'm reading that has nothing to do with my day job. There
are tags, and they're loose — a way to find things again, not a taxonomy. If you
follow along, expect the subject to change.

What stays constant is the habit: write down the thread while I'm following it,
say what I actually think, and link to what sent me there. That's the whole
promise. Welcome to the holes.
